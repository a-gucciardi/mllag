The sky appears blue because of a phenomenon called scattering, which occurs when sunlight interacts with the tiny molecules of gases in the Earth's atmosphere.

When sunlight enters the Earth's atmosphere, it encounters the nitrogen (N2) and oxygen (O2) molecules that make up about 99% of the air. These molecules are much smaller than the wavelength of visible light, so they scatter the shorter wavelengths of light more than the longer wavelengths.

The blue light is scattered in all directions by the tiny molecules, while the longer wavelengths of light, such as red and orange, continue to travel in a straight line. This means that when we look at the sky, our eyes see mostly the blue light that has been scattered by the atmosphere, giving it its blue appearance.

There are a few reasons why the sky appears blue:

1. **Shorter wavelengths scattered more**: The shorter wavelengths of light (such as blue and violet) are scattered more than the longer wavelengths because they have less mass and are more easily deflected.
2. **Size of molecules compared to wavelength**: The size of the nitrogen and oxygen molecules is comparable to the wavelength of blue light, which allows them to scatter it effectively.
3. **Atmospheric conditions**: The scattering effect is more pronounced when there are fewer particles in the atmosphere, such as during clear weather. When there are more particles, like pollution or dust, they can absorb or scatter light in other ways, changing the apparent color of the sky.

This phenomenon is known as Rayleigh scattering, named after the British physicist Lord Rayleigh, who first described it in the late 19th century.
